# Crafting Adventure
In this project, you will design and develop a 3D, Third-Person **Crafting Adventure**. While the player sees magic and exploration, you will be focusing on the underlying architecture—the invisible engines that manage data, handle interactions, and process logic.

### What Defines a Crafting Game System?

A professional crafting game isn't just a collection of scripts; it is a series of interconnected systems. For this project, your architecture must handle:

-   **Resource Management:** How items are defined, collected, and stored.
-   **Inventory Logic:** How the game tracks what the player "owns" across different scenes.
-  **Crafting Recipes:** A data-driven way to define "Ingredient A + Ingredient B = Product C."
-   **World Interaction:** A consistent way for the player to "talk" to the environment (chests, plants, crafting tables).
-   **State & Flow:** Managing the transition between exploration, crafting menus, and progressing through levels.
    

## 🏃 The Development Sprints

You will not build the final game all at once. You will deliver your work in three iterative stages:
1.  Prototype (Rapid): "The 30 Seconds of Fun." A messy, high-energy build. Prove the core loop (Walk -> Pick Up -> Craft) is satisfying.
2.  Prototype (Draft): "The Player Experience." 1–2 minutes of gameplay. This version connects two levels and introduces puzzles. The "game" is here, but the code might still be a bit "spaghetti."
3.  Tech Demo 01: "The Technical Foundation." 2–3 minutes of gameplay. This is where you refactor and optimize. You will replace hard-coded logic with professional Design Patterns (Observer, Factory, Singleton) and ensure the system is modular.

#### A. Brainstorming Your Game

Begin by brainstorming ideas for your crafting game. Consider the following guiding questions to help define your game’s concept:

- What types of crafting interactions will engage players and provide a meaningful challenge?
- Who is the target audience for this game? What characteristics will appeal to them?
- What is the main objective of your game? What actions must players take to succeed?
- What are the win conditions? How does a player achieve success and complete the game?
- What is the theme of your game?

> [!TIP] 
> Consider creating a player persona for your target audience. This optional step can help narrow the scope of your game by identifying who you're designing for and providing insight into preferences, motivations, and behaviors.
> 

#### B. Plan Your Game's Mechanics

To keep your design focused, narrow your game down to one primary mechanic (e.g., resource collection, crafting recipes, environmental interactions). Limiting your game to a single, core mechanic will help you create a more cohesive and manageable gameplay experience.

In the game production pipeline, a key deliverable of the Concept Stage is the **game design brief**. This document summarizes the overall game design, highlighting the essential elements that need to be created and developed. It is crucial for everyone involved in the production to review and fully understand the details and objectives outlined in the brief. This understanding will provide a solid foundation for your work and ensure clarity on what needs to be developed for the prototype.
